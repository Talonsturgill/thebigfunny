# The Angle Taxonomy

The angle is the whole episode. Everything downstream, the script, the staging,
the cast blocking, is execution. A great angle survives mediocre execution. A
weak angle cannot be rescued by anything, and the Phase 3.5 room exists to kill
weak angles before they cost a render.

## The first law

**"X is bad" is not an angle.**

Everyone already knows airlines are bad, rent is high, and insurance is a maze.
Restating a shared grievance is not comedy, it is agreement, and agreement does
not get shared because it tells nobody anything.

An angle is **the one specific detail that does all the work.** Not "hospitals
overcharge." The $629 Band-Aid. Not "streaming got expensive." The tier that
costs more than cable did in the year they told you cable was a ripoff.

If you cannot say the angle in one sentence to someone who has not heard of the
story, and get a reaction, there is no episode.

## The second law: it has to have TEETH

Added 2026-08-02, after case 0002. That episode had a real angle, a clean
institutional villain, two verified counts and a federal filing to end on. It
scored 79.65 and shipped. It was also about a car that would not make a beep,
and no adult would repeat it to another adult.

Passing the first law is not enough. Ask, out loud:

> Would a normal adult say this in a bar, angry, unprompted?

If the worst thing in the story is an INCONVENIENCE, the angle is dead no matter
how specific it is. The show wants stories where somebody is getting hurt in the
wallet on purpose and it is working, where the institution said the quiet part in
public and did not flinch, or where the mechanism is slightly obscene the moment
you describe it plainly.

**This show is for adults.** Crude is the register, not a risk to be managed.
CLAUDE.md permits profanity explicitly and the scoring rubric now caps a
toothless episode at 70 no matter how well made it is. The border to work
against is the ban list, which is slurs, hate, sexual content and harassment,
plus never punching at a private individual. Everything short of that line is
available, and the show has been leaving most of it on the table.

## The seven that land

Every one of these is found by RESEARCH, not by writing. That is deliberate.
The machine is good at finding and terrible at inventing, so the angle room
hunts rather than brainstorms.

### 1. The Euphemism
What they called it, next to what it is. The gap is the joke and it needs no
help.

> "Guest experience adjustment." They took the free bag.
> "Negative patient outcome." He died.
> "Involuntary career transition." Fired, by email, at 6am.

Strongest when quoted verbatim. Never paraphrase a euphemism; the exact
corporate wording is funnier than anything written about it.

### 2. The Number
The figure that breaks a viewer's brain. Works alone, with no commentary.

Requires a real citation and a real comparison unit. "$400" means nothing.
"$400 for the saline. Salt water. In a bag." means everything.

### 3. The Ratio
Two true numbers placed next to each other. No argument, no adjective. The
viewer does the math and the math radicalizes them.

> The CEO's raise. The layoff count. Same slide.

The discipline here is to place and shut up. Any commentary weakens it.

### 4. The Quiet Change
They edited the terms and hoped nobody read it. Timestamped, ideally late on a
Friday.

This one is the most reliably enraging because the concealment is itself the
confession. Show the diff.

### 5. The Straight Face
They announced it PROUDLY. As good news. In a press release, with a graphic.

The comedy is entirely in the sincerity. Do not editorialize; play the press
release straight and let Ray react.

### 6. The Precedent
This was unthinkable five years ago and is now standard, and nobody voted on it.

Requires a real "before" citation, which makes it the most research-expensive
angle and one of the most shareable. Resale fees. Tipping screens on
self-checkout. Subscription hardware in a car you own.

### 7. The Who Benefits
Follow the money exactly ONE step. Not three. One.

One step is a joke. Three steps is a conspiracy theory, and a conspiracy theory
is both unfunny and legally dangerous. If the connection needs a chart, cut it.

## Angle hygiene

**One angle per episode.** Two angles is a video essay. Sixty seconds holds one
idea and the reaction to it.

**The angle must survive the fact-check.** If Phase 2 kills the supporting
document, the angle dies with it. Do not soften a claim to keep an angle alive;
that is the exact move that ends channels. Kill it and take the next story.

**The angle must be about an institution.** If the angle only works by making a
named private individual look stupid, it is out, regardless of how funny it is.

**Check `ledger/bits.json` before locking.** The show must not run the same
angle TYPE more than twice in any seven episodes. Four Numbers in a row and the
show is a spreadsheet with a voice.

## What to do when the day is thin

The NO EMPTY RUNS law applies, so "nothing happened" is not an outcome. In
order:

1. Widen to the other six beats before widening the definition of a story.
2. Take a slightly older story with a NEW document (a filing that just dropped
   on an old grievance is a fresh angle).
3. Take the structural version: not today's fee, but the fee's third increase
   this year, which is a Precedent angle.

Never pad. A 60 second episode built on a 20 second angle is worse than a good
episode about a smaller story.
