# HOW A JOKE IS BUILT

Commissioned by the owner 2026-08-02: *"study the greats and how they think...
I like Dave Chappelle, Russell Brand, Chris Rock, Bill Burr."*

## What this file takes, and what it refuses to take

It takes **mechanics**: how a bit is constructed, escalated, misdirected and
landed. Those are craft, they are documented, and they transfer to any voice.

It does not take their voices, their personas or their material. Two reasons,
and both are load-bearing. Putting a real comedian's act in Ray's mouth is
somebody else's work with the serial numbers filed off. And this show has a
FIXED CAST whose whole value is that you know who is about to speak; Ray doing a
Chris Rock impression is not Ray.

So: steal the engine, never the paint.

## The diagnosis this file exists to fix

Two blind funny-critic reads of case 0003, an hour apart, both returned
`is_it_agreement_not_comedy: true` and `carried_by: FACT`, at 57 and 58 out of
100. The second read, after a rewrite that fixed every STRUCTURAL note:

> "There is exactly one real joke in here... Everything around it is verified
> outrage recited with attitude."

The machine can now find a true, sourced, infuriating fact and stage it
beautifully. **It cannot yet write a joke.** That is the entire remaining gap.

## THE SIX MOVES

### 1. THE ACT-OUT (Burr, Chappelle)
**Do not describe the person. BECOME them.**

Burr's bits live on act-outs; Chappelle plays every character in his stories.
Describing is journalism. Becoming is comedy.

Case 0003 got one third of the way there. "Somebody hit paste twice" was the
only line either critic called a real joke, because it compressed a federal
accuracy violation into a person double-tapping ctrl-V. But it DESCRIBED that
person. The full move is Ray *being* him for two seconds:

> Ray, as the guy: "Yeah, no, that one's in there twice. That's fine."

The show has two mouths and a rig with expressions. It can act out. It never has.

### 2. TENSION, THEN NAME IT (Burr)
Burr takes a position that makes a room anxious, then **acknowledges the tension
exists**, and the audience releases it as a laugh. Commitment to an emotional
point of view is the engine; neutrality kills it.

For us: the tension is that the viewer has been judged by a machine that cannot
count. Do not resolve that tension gently. Name it out loud at its worst.

### 3. HYPER-SPECIFICITY (Rock)
**Specificity plus recognition generates the laugh.** Not "screening companies
are careless". The exact number, the exact word, the exact stupid mechanism.

This is the one move the show already does well, because it is the same instinct
as the fact-check gate. The $629 Band-Aid. The same eviction, twice.

### 4. RULE OF THREE: establish, reinforce, SURPRISE (Rock)
Two items build a pattern, the third breaks it. When reaching for the third, try
four versions: one absurd, one darkly honest, one hyper-specific, and one that
breaks the format itself.

The show has never once used a list of three. It is the cheapest reliable joke
structure in existence and it costs about four seconds.

### 5. PLANT AND PAYOFF / THE CALLBACK (Chappelle, Rock)
Plant something small early, pay it off late. Rock brings a minor character back
to carry the larger point; Chappelle advances a narrative rather than delivering
"and, and, and".

A sixty second show can hold exactly one plant. Use it. The button is the
natural payoff position and is currently spent on a document alone.

### 6. THE REGISTER CLASH (Brand)
Elevated, latinate, ornate language against a low or squalid subject. The gap
between how something is SAID and what it IS.

**The show already owns this and has never exploited it.** The Institution
speaks statute; Ray speaks like a man in a stairwell. That is a Brand-shaped
engine sitting idle. The clause and the swear should collide in the same beat,
not sit in separate ones.

### And the delivery move: THE PREGNANT PAUSE (Chappelle)
Silence at the moment of maximum tension amplifies it. We have `[short pause]`
and a face rig that can hold a reaction. A held reaction shot on a silent Ray is
free runtime and the panel keeps asking for more of it.

## THE ANTI-PATTERNS, all three earned this week

### THE APHORISM IS NOT A JOKE
If a sentence already exists on the internet, it is not writing. The critic on
case 0003:

> "'I'm not the customer. I'm the goddamn product' is a fifteen-year-old
> internet aphorism with a swear stapled on. It is the angle typed out loud."

Banned by construction: "you're the product", "make it make sense", "late-stage
capitalism", "the system is working as designed", "this is fine". **A swear does
not upgrade a cliché.** If the line would get likes as a tweet with no video
attached, it is not a joke, it is a caption.

### CHECK WHICH WAY YOUR IRONY POINTS
The single most useful note either critic gave, and it is not in any comedy book:

> "'$2.25 million / For pressing paste twice' reads as an enormous fine for a
> trivial clerical slip, which accidentally makes RentGrow the sympathetic
> party."

**Before locking a button, ask who the joke makes sympathetic.** If minimising
the mechanism minimises the CRIME, the irony has reversed and the episode is
now arguing for the defendant. Fix it by keeping the harm in frame next to the
smallness of the cause: the trivial act and the person who lost the apartment,
in the same breath.

### NEVER EXPLAIN THE COMPRESSION
"Somebody hit paste twice." is the joke. "That's the whole thing." is the writer
checking the audience got it. Cut the second sentence every time. This audience
is faster than the script.

## THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS

Right now the run finds a fact and stages it. That is backwards for the last
step. Once the angle is locked:

1. **Find the ONE thing to compress.** The stupidest mechanism in the document.
2. **Build the act-out on it.** Who is the person doing the stupid thing, and
   what do they say while doing it?
3. **Plant something in the first fifteen seconds** that the button can pay off.
4. **Find one place for a rule of three.**
5. **Collide the registers once**: statute against a swear, in one beat.
6. **Check which way the irony points.**
7. **Cut every line that explains the line above it.**

## THE BAR

Not "is this true", not "is this outrageous", and not "is this well made". All
three are already gated. The bar is:

> **Is there a JOKE in here, or is this a fact with a tone of voice?**

Two independent reads have now answered "a fact with a tone of voice". Until
that answer changes, the episode is not funny, however well it renders.
