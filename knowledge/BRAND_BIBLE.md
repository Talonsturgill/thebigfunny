# The Big Funny — Brand Bible

A brand is not a logo. It is the set of things a viewer can recognise in half a
second with the sound off, and the promise those things make.

## The promise

> **We didn't make this up. We have the filing.**

That is the whole brand in seven words, and every decision below serves it. The
show is not "angry guy reacts to news"; the internet has ten thousand of those
and none of them can prove anything. The show is **a case file that happens to be
funny**, and the receipt at the end is the proof.

This is also why the brand is not slick. Slick reads as marketing, marketing
reads as a pitch, and a pitch is the thing the Institution does. Our visual
language is the language of the paperwork used against you: manila, carbon
copy, rubber stamps, docket numbers, redaction. We took the bureaucracy's own
kit and pointed it back.

## The name

**THE BIG FUNNY.** Dumb on purpose.

It commits to zero topics, which means the show can chase anything in America
without a rebrand. It is also disarming: a channel called "Corporate Accountability
Daily" gets argued with, and a channel called The Big Funny gets watched. The
seriousness is smuggled in under a stupid name, which is the oldest trick in
satire and it still works.

Never abbreviate to TBF in reader-facing copy. It is always the whole dumb name.

## The case number

**Every episode is a case.** `CASE No. 0001`, incrementing forever, on the title
card and the end card.

This is the single most important brand device and it is doing four jobs:

1. It makes the archive feel like a **record** rather than a feed. Case 400
   implies 399 before it, and implies somebody kept them.
2. It sets the register in one glyph. This is a file, not a rant.
3. It is the natural handle for a comment section: "case 212 broke me".
4. It is inherited honestly. The upstream publication ran a public docket of
   tracked decisions, and this is the same instinct pointed at comedy.

A case number is never reused and never skipped. A gap in the numbering would be
the show lying about its own record on the one axis where its whole credibility
sits. In practice every case ends in a delivered episode, because a gate failure
sends the run back to fix the work rather than ending it; a number is only ever
spent without an episode behind it in a genuine outage, which is an incident.

## Colour

Built on the engine's existing tokens rather than a parallel palette, so the
brand and the art library cannot drift.

| Token | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `INK` | `#101423` | Type, linework. Already the library's ink. |
| `MANILA` | `#E3D4B0` | The folder. The show's ground. |
| `CARBON` | `#4A5A66` | The Institution. Already `PaperOfficeBG`'s dark anchor. |
| `STAMP` | `#C0392B` | The stamp, the denial, the damage. Used ONCE per episode. |
| `HIGHLIGHT` | `#FFE24A` | The damning number. Already in the library. |
| `PAPER` | `#F2EADA` | Documents, captions. |

**The one-stamp rule.** `STAMP` red appears exactly once in an episode, on the
thing that matters most. A second use halves the first. This is the discipline
that keeps the palette from becoming decoration.

Ray is warm against `CARBON`. The Institution is `CARBON` and never warm. If a
frame has no cold and no warm in it, the episode has forgotten what it is about.

## Type

`Arial Black` is what the engine has and it is genuinely right here: heavy,
condensed, unfussy, American, and it reads at thumbnail size where the platform
actually decides. Do not chase a fancier face; a bureaucratic show should look
like it was set by somebody with no design budget and good instincts.

- **Headlines / the wordmark**: Arial Black, all caps, tight tracking.
- **Documents and captions**: Arial, normal weight. The document must read as a
  document, not as a title card.
- **The case number**: Arial, letter-spaced wide, small. It is a serial, not a
  headline.

## The five signature moves

These are the recognisable-in-half-a-second things. An episode that skips them
is off brand even if every line lands.

1. **Cold open, no logo.** The first two seconds are the fact, flat, no setup.
   Branding before the hook is how a short-form video loses its audience, and no
   brand survives not being watched.
2. **The wordmark stamps.** Around 2s, once, hard, with a slight rotation and
   ink bleed. It never fades in. A stamp is a verdict.
3. **The highlighter.** The single worst number in the episode gets a marker
   swipe, hand-drawn and slightly overshooting the text. Once.
4. **The button is the receipt.** The real document, on screen, legible. The
   stamp lands on it. This is the show's whole reason to exist.
5. **The end card.** Case number, the promise line, nothing else. No subscribe
   animation, no end screen clutter. The restraint is the flex.

## Voice in copy

Same rules as the script. Titles are the angle, not a description.

- Good: `CASE No. 0031 — The Ambulance Was Out Of Network. The Ambulance They Sent.`
- Bad: `Insurance Company Denies Ambulance Claim (INSANE)`

No clickbait punctuation. No all-caps screaming in a title, because the wordmark
already screams and two things screaming is noise. No emoji, ever, anywhere. The
show is deadpan and deadpan does not use a crying-laughing face.

## What the brand is not

- Not political. No red-vs-blue in the palette by accident: `STAMP` red is a
  rubber stamp, and it lands on corporations far more often than anything else.
- Not slick, not gradient, not glassy, not a tech brand.
- Not a personality channel. Ray is a character, not a host, and there is never a
  real face or a real name attached to the show's voice.
- Not merchandise-first. If a bit only exists to sell a shirt, cut it.
